## PLAINTIFF JOURNEY (9 steps)

### Screen P1 — "Is small claims the right court?"

**Body (from Bailey, verified against SB 1330 / § 1-2301):**
- Small claims court handles claims for a money judgment or for the return of personal property.
- **Claim limits:** If your claim arose **on or after July 1, 2026**, the limit is **$15,000**. If your claim arose **before July 1, 2026**, the limit is **$5,000**.
- You cannot split one larger claim into several smaller claims to get around the limit.
- The court cannot award punitive damages or damages for pain and suffering.
- No attorneys represent parties in the courtroom, and there are no jury trials. A judge (magistrate) decides the case.
- File in the county where the defendant lives or where the events happened.
- Other courts: Magistrate court hears cases up to $10,000; district court hears cases over $10,000. You can research the differences — each court is governed by its own Supreme Court rules: https://isc.idaho.gov/rules-procedure
- This guide cannot tell you which court is right for your situation. For advice about your case, talk to a lawyer (see HELP screen).

**Official links:** Idaho Code § 1-2301 · I.R.S.C.A. (full rules)

### Screen P2 — "Getting ready to file"

**Body (from Bailey):**
- Before filing, it helps to gather:
  - The defendant's name(s), address(es), and phone number(s)
  - The date your claim arose and the amount
  - Any supporting documents
- Naming a business defendant correctly matters. If the business is a sole proprietorship, list the owner's name and the business name. The Idaho Secretary of State's office can help you find a business owner's name.
- **The most common form mistake: leaving something blank.** Fill in every part of the form — write "N/A," "none," or "unknown" if needed.
- **Deadlines exist.** Different kinds of cases have different filing deadlines (statutes of limitations). If you miss your deadline, you won't be able to file your claim. To know the deadline for your case, ask a lawyer or read the state law: https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title5/T5CH2/
- Get the official forms: https://courtselfhelp.idaho.gov/Forms/claims (fillable, in Word and PDF). You can also use Guide & File, an online tool that prepares your forms: http://guideandfile.idaho.gov
- The CAO can clerically review your forms before you file — optional but recommended. You're welcome to file without review.

**Official links:** Forms page · Guide & File · Title 5, Ch. 2 (deadlines)

### Screen P3 — "Filing your claim and paying the fee"

**Body (from Bailey):**
- **Three ways to file:** in person, by mail, or online through eFile & Serve (https://idaho.tylerhost.net/ofsweb). Businesses filing in a county with e-filing **must** e-file.
- **In person in Bannock County:** Court Records, Room 108, Bannock County Courthouse, 624 E Center St, Pocatello. Bring the Claim, Summons, and Civil Case Information Sheet (optional for small claims), plus the **$69 filing fee**.
- The clerk will take your forms, open your case, and hand back annotated copies for every party — copies for your records and copies to serve on each defendant — plus a defendant response/information packet for each defendant.
- If you file online, you should receive the annotated copies and defendant packet by email.
- **Other costs to expect:** certified-mail service $10.10 · sheriff's service $50 (Bannock County) · copies of filed documents about $1/page. You total the claim, filing fee, and service fees into the grand total requested from the court for a money judgment.
- **Can't afford the fee?** You can ask for a fee waiver using the Supreme Court forms (Motion and Affidavit for Fee Waiver + proposed Order). In Bannock County, submit the fee waiver documents together with your Claim and Summons; the clerk reviews and sends them to the judge. If granted, your case is filed and the clerk emails your copies. If denied, you'll need to pay the fee. Decisions often take a few days up to a week, sometimes two. It is your responsibility to monitor your case status.

**Official links:** § 1-2303 · Fee waiver forms (courtselfhelp.idaho.gov)

### Screen P4 — "Serving the defendant"

**Body (from Bailey / Supreme Court instructions):**
- Each defendant must be served (given) copies of: the Summons, Small Claim, blank Answer, and Instructions for Defendant.
- **Someone 18 or older — not you — must serve the defendant.** Options and typical costs:
  1. **Certified mail through the clerk** — $10.10. Warning: if the defendant refuses to sign, or someone else signs, you must serve again using personal service. The clerk will notify you.
  2. **Sheriff's office** — $50 in Bannock County. Tell them exactly where to find the defendant. The sheriff completes the Affidavit of Service.
  3. **Professional process server** — cost varies. Some will locate a hard-to-find defendant for a fee.
  4. **Any adult not involved in the case** — generally free.
- **Proof of service:** After serving, the server fills out the Affidavit of Service and returns it to you. You must file it with the clerk (or bring it to trial). **The deadline to serve and file is 30 days in most cases; some courts allow up to 90. Ask the clerk about your deadline.** ⚠️ VERIFY Bannock's exact deadline for the app.
- **If service fails** (wrong address, missed deadline): you can file your claim again if you're still within your claim's deadline. If you refile within 6 months of your first filing, you may not have to pay another filing fee.
- If the defendant is never served, the court can dismiss the case "without prejudice" (meaning it can be refiled). The clerk gives at least 14 days' notice first.

**Official links:** § 1-2304 · I.R.S.C.A. Rule 10

### Screen P5 — "The defendant's 21 days"

**Body (from Bailey):**
- After being served, the defendant has **21 days** to file an Answer with the court.
- **If the defendant answers:** the court orders both parties to mediation (see next step).
- **If the defendant does not answer:** you can ask for a default judgment. In Bannock County: (1) start the case, (2) serve the defendant, (3) wait the full 21 days, then (4) file the **Affidavit for Default, of Competence, Non-Military Service, and Amount Due (form CAO SC 5-1)**.
- Even in a default, the judge must be satisfied by the evidence supporting the claim.
- Counterclaims are not allowed in small claims. A defendant who has their own claim against you files a separate case.
- If you (the plaintiff) don't show up for a hearing, the court may dismiss your case.

**Official links:** § 1-2303 · I.R.S.C.A. Rules 4, 5 · Form CAO SC 5-1

### Screen P6 — "Mediation" *(new step — Bannock practice)*

**Body (from Bailey):**
- In most Bannock County cases, when the defendant files an Answer, the parties are **ordered to mediation**: a meeting with a neutral, third-party mediator to try to reach an agreement together.
- Timing: the order to attend mediation is created the same day the Answer is filed (the judge typically signs it the same day or the following Monday). Mediation is generally due **three Fridays** after the order is created (if the Answer is filed on a Thursday or Friday, that upcoming Friday doesn't count).
- If you reach an agreement, your case may end there — no trial.
- If mediation reaches an impasse, the court issues a scheduling order for a hearing, and you'll receive instructions on submitting exhibits and preparing for the informal trial.
- Many cases settle in mediation. ⚠️ VERIFY: exact description of how mediation is scheduled (in person? Zoom? who the mediators are?) — ask Bailey.

**Official links:** § 1-2303(2)

### Screen P7 — "Getting ready for your trial"

**Body (from Bailey / Supreme Court instructions, quoted with attribution):**
- If a hearing is scheduled, you'll receive a **scheduling order for small claims trial**. It contains hearing information, trial exhibit instructions, and deadlines. Review it carefully — court staff cannot legally interpret it for you.
- The Supreme Court's official guide "Get Ready for Your Small Claims Trial" covers preparation: https://courtselfhelp.idaho.gov (under Small Claims).
- **Copies:** for exhibits, generally bring a copy for each party and the judge.
- **Witnesses:** you may have witnesses speak at trial. If a witness can't attend, the official instructions describe written statements. Recordings (audio/video): talk to the clerk as soon as possible and ask whether to bring your own equipment.
- **Interpreter:** contact the clerk if you or a witness needs help with English or has hearing problems. The clerk can send a **free interpreter** to your trial. Friends and relatives are not allowed to interpret.
- **Remote appearance:** Bannock County small claims hearings can be remote, or a party can appear remotely if requested — file a **Motion and Order to Appear by Telephone or Zoom**.
- **Can't attend on your date?** Contact the clerk. If you have an urgent reason to change the date, file a **Motion and Order to Continue at least 2 weeks before** your trial.
- Trials are informal (I.R.S.C.A. Rule 11). The judge may allow telephonic appearance and may adjourn to allow more relevant evidence.

**Official links:** I.R.S.C.A. Rules 8, 11 · "Get Ready for Your Small Claims Trial" (courtselfhelp.idaho.gov)

### Screen P8 — "Your trial and the decision"

**Body (quoted/adapted from Supreme Court instructions, per Bailey):**
- **Arrive early.** Allow time for security and finding your courtroom (hearings are generally at the Bannock County Courthouse, 624 E Center St, Courtroom 218). Bring your forms.
- Wait for the court to call your case. **The judge asks the plaintiff to speak first** — your chance to say the most important reasons that support your case. It's OK to bring notes and read from them.
- Give the judge copies of your proof: contracts, receipts, cancelled checks, invoices, statements, photos.
- Then the defendant explains their side and may show evidence and bring witnesses.
- **Courtroom basics:** phone off, hat off, no food/gum/drinks. Stay calm and polite. Don't interrupt. Don't speak until the judge asks. If you don't understand something, say "I don't understand." Don't bring children unless they can sit quietly for a few hours.
- **The decision:** the judge decides many cases at the end of the trial and gives both parties a copy of the judgment. If the judge decides later, the judgment comes in the mail.

**Official links:** I.R.S.C.A. Rules 11, 12

### Screen P9 — "After the decision: appeals and collecting"

**Body (from Bailey):**
- **Appeals.** Either party can appeal within a strict **30 days** of the judgment by filing a Notice of Appeal with the clerk and paying an **$81** fee. You can appeal if you lost, or if you won but received less than you asked for. Appeals are more formal than small claims — the parties can have lawyers. The appeal is a new trial (see glossary: "trial de novo"). If the other party appeals, the clerk sends you the Notice.
- **If you won:** the court orders the loser to pay or return property, **but the court does not collect for you.** You generally must wait out the 30-day appeal window before formal collection. If they don't pay, you can ask the court for a writ (garnish wages, levy bank accounts, place a property lien). See the official brochure "Collecting on Your Small Claims Judgment": courtselfhelp.idaho.gov/publications (under Small Claims). Once paid in full, file a **Satisfaction of Judgment** with the clerk.
- **If you lost:** pay the amount or return the property ordered. If you disagree, you have 30 days to appeal (above). Ignoring a judgment allows the winner to seek court-enforced collection.
- **If you owe and can't pay all at once:** debtors can arrange voluntary installment agreements with creditors. If collection begins, a **Claim of Exemption** must be filed within **14 calendar days** to protect certain assets (for example, social security, child support, limited home equity). The CAO, the self-help website, and legal resources can help. (Not an exhaustive list.)

**Official links:** I.R.S.C.A. Rules 14, 15 · §§ 1-2311, 1-2312 · Collecting brochure

## DEFENDANT BRANCH

### Screen D1 — "You've been sued — what now?"

**Body (from Supreme Court instructions, per Bailey):**
- The person who sued you is the Plaintiff; you are the Defendant.
- First question the official instructions ask: **Do you agree that you owe the money or the property belongs to the Plaintiff?** Your response is the Answer form you were served with.
- **You have 21 days from the date you were served to file your Answer.** If you don't, the judge may grant the Plaintiff a default judgment.
- Small claims is informal: no attorneys in the courtroom, no jury. A judge decides.
- Counterclaims aren't allowed — if you have your own claim against the Plaintiff, you file a separate case.
- For advice about your situation, talk to a lawyer (HELP screen).

### Screen D2 — "Filing your Answer"

**Body (from Supreme Court instructions, per Bailey):**
- Three ways to complete and file:
  - **A. Guide & File (online):** prepares your forms and lets you e-file: http://guideandfile.idaho.gov
  - **B. eFile & Serve:** https://idaho.tylerhost.net/ofsweb (businesses in e-filing counties must e-file)
  - **C. Print and file in person:** forms at https://courtselfhelp.idaho.gov/small-claims or at the courthouse. Sign and date, make at least two copies (one for you, one for each Plaintiff), and take them to the court where the Claim was filed.
- After you answer, the court orders mediation (see Screen P6 — same process for both sides) and, if needed, schedules a trial and mails both parties the notice.
- If you don't attend your trial, the judge may grant a default judgment as if you hadn't answered.
- To prepare, see "Get Ready for Your Small Claims Trial — Defendant" on courtselfhelp.idaho.gov.

## Screen GLOSSARY — "Court words in plain language"

*(All definitions are Bailey's, approved as written.)*

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Plaintiff | The person or business who files the lawsuit. |
| Defendant | The person or business being sued. |
| Complaint (Claim) | The written form that starts the case and states the money or property owed. |
| Answer | The defendant's written reply to the claim. |
| Service / Serve | Giving official copies of court papers to the defendant. |
| Proof of service | A signed paper showing the defendant received the court documents. |
| Default judgment | A win for the plaintiff if the defendant fails to show up or answer. |
| Without prejudice | Dismissal that allows the plaintiff to refile the case later. |
| Judgment | The final decision by the court about who wins and how much is owed. |
| Execution / Writ | A court order used to take property or money from the loser to pay the debt. |
| Trial de novo ("new appeal trial") | A brand-new trial restarting an appeal from small claims. |
| Magistrate | The judge who hears the small claims case in an Idaho magistrate court. |
| Venue | The correct county court location to file the case. |
| Mediation | A meeting with a neutral helper to try and settle the dispute before seeing a judge. |
