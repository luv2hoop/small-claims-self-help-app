After the decision: appeals and collecting

Appeals. Either party can appeal within a strict 30 days of the judgment by filing a Notice of Appeal with the clerk and paying an $81 fee. You can appeal if you lost, or if you won but received less than you asked for. Appeals are more formal than small claims — the parties can have lawyers. The appeal is a new trial (see the glossary: “trial de novo”). If the other party appeals, the clerk sends you the Notice.

If you won: the court orders the loser to pay or return property, but the court does not collect for you. You generally must wait out the 30-day appeal window before formal collection. If they don't pay, you can ask the court for a writ (garnish wages, levy bank accounts, place a property lien). See the official brochure, Collecting on Your Small Claims Judgment. Once paid in full, file a Satisfaction of Judgment with the clerk.

If you lost: pay the amount or return the property ordered. If you disagree, you have 30 days to appeal (above). Ignoring a judgment allows the winner to seek court-enforced collection.

If you owe and can't pay all at once: debtors can arrange voluntary installment agreements with creditors. If collection begins, a Claim of Exemption must be filed within 14 calendar days to protect certain assets (for example, social security, child support, limited home equity). The CAO, the self-help website, and legal resources can help. (Not an exhaustive list.)
